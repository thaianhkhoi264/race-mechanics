# Glossary

What players mean by the words they use, mapped to the Global (English) game term this assistant
answers with. Grouped by topic; each entry lists the other names people type (JP romaji, slang,
abbreviations) so a question in any of them finds it. Numbers and formulas live in the mechanics
notes, not here.

Terms collected with the help of the community glossary at uma.guide
(https://uma.guide/guides/glossary); the definitions below are our own.

Answering rule: always use the Global term. If the asker used a JP or slang term, give it once in
brackets, e.g. "Rushed (kakari in JP)".

# Running styles

## Running style (strategy)
Also: strategy, style, kyakushitsu. Which part of the field a runner aims to sit in before the
final push. There are four styles plus Runaway, which is a Front Runner variant unlocked by a
skill. The style decides HP use, position-keeping behaviour and which style-locked skills can fire.

## Front Runner (JP: nige)
Also: nige, front, FR. Wants first place from the gate and tries to keep it. Spends HP fastest in
the early race and is the style most exposed to being overtaken late.

## Runaway (JP: oonige, great escape)
Also: oonige, great escape, runaway. An extreme Front Runner that opens a large gap early. Burns
even more HP than a normal Front Runner; a Runaway in the lobby drags the whole pace up.

## Pace Chaser (JP: senkou)
Also: senkou, pace, PC. Sits just behind the leaders, roughly 2nd to 4th, and moves up in the late
race. Usually the most forgiving style.

## Late Surger (JP: sashi)
Also: sashi, betweener, late, LS. Runs mid-pack, roughly 4th to 7th, and makes its move from the
final corner on.

## End Closer (JP: oikomi)
Also: oikomi, closer, end, EC. Stays at the back and relies on a strong finish. Cheapest HP use
early, but needs room and acceleration late.

## Frontline and backline
Shorthand for where styles usually run: the frontline is Front Runners (and often Pace Chasers),
the backline is Late Surgers and End Closers (and sometimes Pace Chasers). Many skill conditions
and debuffs are aimed at one of the two groups.

# Race states and modes

## Rushed (JP: kakari)
Also: kakari, rushing, temptation. A runner loses her composure early in the race, uses extra HP
and behaves like a more forward style until it wears off. Wit lowers the chance; some skills cure
it. The mechanics notes have the exact numbers.

## Position Keep (PosKeep)
Also: poskeep, position keeping. The early and mid-race logic that keeps each runner near her
style's usual place by switching between the modes below. Ends partway through the race.

## Pace-Down Mode (PDM)
Also: pdm, pace down. A position-keeping mode for runners behind the leader: when she is closer to
the front than her style wants, she eases off. Saves HP but can cost position.

## Pacemaker
The runner the position-keeping logic measures everyone against, normally the leading Front
Runner. Who it is changes how the whole lobby runs the early race.

## Overtake mode and Normal mode
Overtake mode: a runner actively trying to pass someone ahead (speed-up / overtake behaviour for
leaders). Normal mode: just holding the lane and pace.

## No-Zone (Normal Zone)
Also: no zone. The stretch just before the first corner where position-keeping modes are switched
off and everyone runs in normal mode.

## Under Pressure (being overtaken)
Also: under pressure, being passed. The state of a runner that someone in overtake mode is
targeting; some skills and debuffs check for it.

## Dueling (JP: oikurabe)
Also: oikurabe, compete fight, showdown, duel. Two runners side by side on the final straight push
each other, gaining speed and acceleration from Guts.

## Spot Struggle
Also: lead competition, competing for the lead. Front Runners fighting for first place early in
the race; both spend extra HP while it lasts.

## Last Spurt
Also: spurt, last spurt state. From the start of late-race the runner decides how hard to push for
the rest of the race based on the HP she has left. Not the same as the last-spurt *phase* of the
course.

## Speed Carryover
Also: carryover. A speed skill that fires shortly before late-race and is still running when the
last spurt begins, so part of its boost lands in the spurt.

## Spurt Rate and Survival Rate
Two different simulator numbers. Spurt rate: how often the runner has enough HP to push at full
spurt speed. Survival rate: how often she finishes without running out of HP and dropping to
minimum speed.

## Start Dash
Also: start, gate. The first moments after the gate opens, when every runner accelerates from a
standstill. A slow reaction is a late start.

## HP
Also: health, stamina bar. The resource every runner spends while running; Stamina sets how much
she has. Running out late forces her down to minimum speed.

# Race-day slang and team tactics

## Block
Also: blocked, boxed in. A runner directly in front (or beside) stops a runner from moving up or
changing lanes. Some skills need it, many runs are lost to it.

## Bowling Ball
A runner who runs out of HP in front of others, slows sharply and blocks whoever is behind her.

## Kidnapping
Also: kidnapped. A frontline runner starts badly, ends up behind the backline and has to run the
race from the wrong end of the field.

## Wide Swing (Narnia)
Also: narnia, going wide. Taking a wide line through the final corner, which adds distance before
the straight.

## Rabbit (sacrifice front, slingshot)
Also: sacrifice front, slingshot. A Runaway (or Front Runner) entered to set a fast pace for the
lobby, usually expected to fade late so a teammate benefits.

## Chariot
A team shape: two Front Runners plus a third runner with high Wit who uses them to get into
pace-up and stay near the front.

## Bermuda Triangle
Three runners whose acceleration skills trigger independently, so somebody is likely to win the
final-straight scramble.

## Ace and Debuffer
Ace: the team member built to actually win. Debuffer: a member built mainly to hit opponents with
debuff skills (HP drains, speed and accel reductions) to help the Ace.

## Solo Ace (oshi, double dom)
Also: oshi, double dom. A team with one Ace and two Debuffers supporting her.

## Win Condition (wincon)
Also: wincon. What has to go right for a runner to win, e.g. "be 3rd-4th when the final corner
starts so her accel skill fires".

## Final Corner Velocity Stacking (C4, velo bomb)
Also: c4, velo bomb, velocity stacking. Building so several speed skills fire together around the
final corner and raise target speed in one burst.

# Race structure and speed

## Race phases: early-race, mid-race, final leg, last spurt
Also: phase, final leg. The course is split into early-race, mid-race, late-race (also called the
final leg) and the last-spurt phase near the end. Many skill conditions name a phase.

## Section
One twenty-fourth of the course. Skill conditions and the random speed roll use sections.

## Length (bashin, L)
Also: bashin, L, lengths. The distance unit for gaps between runners; 1 length = 2.5 m, as in
the simulators.

## Speed terms: Velocity, Current Speed, Target Speed, Base Target Speed, Top Speed, Acceleration
Also: velocity, current speed, target speed, top speed, accel. Target speed is what the runner is
trying to reach; current speed is what she is doing right now and moves toward the target at her
acceleration. "Velocity" skills raise the target; acceleration skills make her get there faster.
Top speed is the target speed in the last spurt.

## Field of View (FoV, vision)
Also: fov, vision. How far ahead and how wide a runner "sees" other runners. Affects blocking and
overtaking; vision skills widen it.

# Skills

## Skill types by colour: white, gold, unique, green, recovery, debuff
White: normal skill. Gold: the stronger version that replaces its white. Unique: a character's own
skill (an inherited copy is weaker). Green: passive stat bonus when a condition holds (track,
weather, season, ...). Recovery: restores HP. Debuff: hurts other runners.

## Skill types by effect: velocity, acceleration, navigation, vision, gambling
Velocity (speed) skills raise target speed; acceleration skills raise acceleration; navigation
skills change lane movement; vision skills widen field of view. "Gambling" (gamba) is slang for an
acceleration skill whose activation point is random and therefore unreliable.

## Activate (proc)
Also: proc, trigger, fire. A skill activating in a race. A skill can fail its activation check
(Wit) or never meet its condition.

## Skill Points (SP) and Hint
Also: SP, hint. Skill points buy skills during a Career; a hint for a skill (from events or
training) makes it cheaper.

# Runner, track and course

## Aptitude
Also: apt, aptitude grade. A runner's grade (G to S) for each ground type, distance and style.
Low aptitude makes her slower or weaker in that setting.

## Distance categories: Sprint, Mile, Medium, Long
Sprint up to 1400 m, Mile 1401-1800 m, Medium 1801-2400 m, Long 2401 m and up.

## Track terms: ground type, ground condition, terrain, track conditions
Ground type: turf or dirt. Ground condition: firm, good, soft or heavy. Terrain and track
conditions together are everything about the course on race day: ground, weather and season.

## Course parts: gate, clubhouse turn, backstretch, far turn, homestretch, lane, furlong
Gate: the start. Clubhouse turn: the corner just after the start. Backstretch: the straight on the
far side. Far turn: the corner(s) before the home straight. Homestretch: the final straight. Lane:
sideways position on the track. Furlong: about 200 m.

## Mood
Also: motivation, condition. A runner's mood (from Awful to Great) scales her stats on race day.

# Game modes and training

## Champions Meeting (CM, chanmi)
Also: CM, chanmi. The monthly PvP event on a fixed course; teams of three race other players'
teams. Most questions here are about a specific CM.

## Team Trials (TT)
Also: TT. The asynchronous PvP mode where your team races other players' teams across distances.

## League of Heroes (LoH)
Also: LoH. A ranked PvP event with its own rules and course.

## Legend Races, Daily Races, Exhibition
PvE race modes for rewards and practice.

## Career, Trainee, Veteran
Career: one training run of a runner. Trainee: the runner being trained. Veteran: a finished
runner, used for racing and as a parent.

## Inspiration, Parent, Grandparent, Spark
Also: sparks, factors. Veterans chosen as parents (and their parents) pass stats, aptitudes and
skills to a trainee through sparks, which trigger at inspiration events.

## Support Card, Bond, Friendship Training, Chain Event
Support cards are the deck that shapes a Career; bond is the gauge with each card's character;
friendship training is the boosted training at high bond; chain events are a card's event series.

## Career conditions
Status effects during a Career, good (e.g. Fast Learner: skills cost less) or bad (e.g. Migraine,
Night Owl, Slacker); the bad ones can be cured.

## Scenarios: URA Finale, Unity Cup (Aoharu), Trackblazer, Grand Concert
Also: URA, aoharu, unity cup, trackblazer. The Career scenarios, each with its own rules and
mechanics.

# Tools and general slang

## Umalator and Kachisim
Also: umalator, calculator, kachisim. Community race simulators for comparing skills and builds;
this assistant's length unit and several formulas follow them.

## Makaizo
Training a runner for a distance, ground or style she has very low aptitude for.

## Slang: broken, glue, high roll
Broken: overpowered. Glue: a weak Veteran. High roll: a lucky random outcome (a skill firing at the
best moment, a great Career).

## Costume and alternate versions
Also: alt, version, OG. Alternate versions of a character (Summer, Halloween, Christmas, New Year,
Valentine, Wedding, Festival and others) are separate cards with their own aptitudes and skills.
"Original" (OG) is the base version.
